# Storing Wiktionary text: licence obligations

Ticket: [02-wiktionary-licence-obligations](../tickets/02-wiktionary-licence-obligations.md) · Map: [defined-term](../map.md) · Researched 2026-11-14 from a cloud session · Type: `wayfinder:research`

**This is a practical reading of primary sources, not legal advice.** Items a lawyer should confirm are in section 8; anything not verified against a primary source is marked UNVERIFIED.

## Recommendation

Store and show Wiktionary definitions, but treat them as third-party CC BY-SA content that stays visibly separate from user-written text: the licence allows commercial use, snapshots in our own database, and reformatting, and asks for three things in return: (1) credit that links to the specific Wiktionary page and to the licence wherever the text appears, (2) a note that we excerpted or changed it, and (3) that anything we *adapt* from it be offered under CC BY-SA, which we avoid by never merging, editing in place, or rewording dictionary text (user definitions shown beside it are a separate work, not an adaptation, so share-alike does not reach them). Suggested credit wording: the short form, shown next to every dictionary definition on every surface (grid cell, tooltip, picker row, badge), is "From Wiktionary · CC BY-SA 4.0" with "Wiktionary" linked to `https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/<term>` and "CC BY-SA 4.0" linked to `https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/`; the full form, shown once per term in the details view under the dictionary group, is "Dictionary definitions excerpted from the Wiktionary entry “<term>” (https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/<term>), retrieved <date>. Text by Wiktionary contributors, available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 license (CC BY-SA 4.0). Excerpted and reformatted; not endorsed by Wiktionary or the Wikimedia Foundation. Definitions written by Perspectize users are not part of that entry."; pronunciation audio gets its own line built from that file's own data, "Pronunciation: <author> via Wikimedia Commons, <licence name>" with the author, the Commons file page and the licence deed linked (for example "Pronunciation: Dvortygirl via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0"), because each audio file has its own licence, author and sometimes a different licence family (we saw CC BY-SA 3.0, CC BY-SA 4.0 and CC BY 3.0 US in three words). Make dictionary rows read-only (a user's "write my own" starts from a blank box, not from the dictionary text), store the source URL, retrieval date and licence name/URL per entry and the author, licence and file URL per audio file, return those fields through GraphQL, carve the Wiktionary text out of our own Terms of Service ("no copying" clauses must not cover it), do not store the API's `example` strings until someone checks them (one observed example is a Bible quotation, which Wiktionary may carry under fair use rather than CC BY-SA), and credit with the plain word "Wiktionary" rather than a Wikimedia logo. The same obligations apply whether we store a snapshot or fetch live, and the CC BY-SA 4.0 text has no NonCommercial element, so calling Perspectize commercial changes nothing for Wiktionary's own text.

## 1. Which licence applies

- **Wiktionary text:** Wiktionary:Copyrights says entry text is "dual-licensed to the public under both the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International License (CC-BY-SA) and the GNU Free Documentation License (GFDL)". That page carries a caution that it "is outdated. It dates to 2002 and was copied from Wikipedia", so use it as supporting evidence only. The current Wikimedia Terms of Use (in effect since 7 June 2023) say contributors license text under CC BY-SA 4.0 and the GFDL and "Reusers may comply with either license or both." (7(a)). The live Wiktionary page footer (checked in the HTML of `en.wiktionary.org/wiki/worship`, 2026-11-14) reads "Definitions and other text are available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike License; additional terms may apply" and links to `creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/`.
- **Older text may still be 3.0.** Wikimedia's legal note on the 2023 move says new additions are licensed 4.0, edits "will often include a mixture of new additions as well as text that was previously added by other users, including text that was contributed under CC BY-SA 3.0", and that "CC BY-SA 3.0 is compatible with CC BY-SA 4.0 and later versions of the same license. After the Terms of Use are modified to use version 4.0, an article may be used under the 4.0 version of the license according to the attribution requirements in the Wikimedia Terms of Use."
- **The Free Dictionary API's label:** every response we probed (`worship`, `hello`, `bliss`, `mercy`) carries `"license":{"name":"CC BY-SA 3.0","url":"https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0"}` and `"sourceUrls":["https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/<word>"]`. The API's site (`dictionaryapi.dev`) and its GitHub README state no terms, licence, attribution rule, rate window or commercial policy. The 3.0 label is the API's own; the licensor is the Wiktionary community. The API probably built from a dump older than mid-2023 (UNVERIFIED), which would explain 3.0.
- **Practical reading:** the two versions have near-identical attribution and share-alike rules (differences below), so one credit satisfies both. Link the licence to the 4.0 deed, as Wiktionary's own footer does and as Wikimedia's legal note permits, and store the API's 3.0 label too. Whether text that is exclusively 3.0 needs a 3.0 link is a question for the lawyer (section 8).
- **Differences between 3.0 and 4.0 that matter here:**
  - 4.0 s3(a)(1)(B) says to "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material"; 3.0 s4(c) only requires a credit for adaptations.
  - 3.0 defines "Collection" and says a Collection "will not be considered an Adaptation" (s1(a), s1(b)); 4.0 drops the term but the CC FAQ says collections are allowed under 4.0 too.
  - 3.0 s7(a): "terminate automatically upon any breach"; 4.0 s6(b) reinstates rights if the violation is "cured within 30 days of Your discovery of the violation". Fix attribution problems fast either way.
  - 4.0 covers sui generis database rights (s4); 3.0 does not explicitly.

## 2. What attribution must look like

**What the licence requires** (CC BY-SA 4.0 s3(a)(1)): if we Share the material (including in modified form) we must retain, *if supplied by the Licensor with the material*, (i) identification of the creators, (ii) a copyright notice, (iii) a notice referring to the licence, (iv) a notice referring to the disclaimer of warranties, (v) "a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably practicable"; we must "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material"; and we must "indicate the Licensed Material is licensed under this Public License, and include the text of, or the URI or hyperlink to, this Public License". Section 3(a)(2) lets us do this "in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context", and "it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information".

**What Wikimedia says counts as credit for Wiktionary text:** Terms of Use 7(g): when you reuse a text page you agree to attribute the authors "Through hyperlink (where possible) or URL to the page or pages that you are reusing (since each page has a history page that lists all contributors, authors and editors)", or a link to a stable copy that credits authors equivalently, or "a list of all authors". Wiktionary:Copyrights says the same in older words: "a conspicuous link back to the home of the article". Wikipedia's reuse page gives a model notice, which we adapt: "This article uses material from the Wikipedia article "X", which is released under the Creative Commons Attribution-Share-Alike License 4.0." and suggests the licence link point to "a local copy of the CC BY-SA 4.0 text on your server". I found no equivalent Wiktionary-specific reuse page (the URL `Wiktionary:Reusing_Wiktionary_content` returns 404), so Wikipedia's page is the closest Wikimedia model.

**What that means for us:**

1. **Link to the term's own page**, `https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/<term>`, not to the Wiktionary home page. That page's history is the author list. The map already makes that page the term's canonical URL.
2. **Name the licence and link it** every time, in the short form too.
3. **Say we excerpted or changed it.** The CC FAQ: "You must also indicate if you have modified the work—for example, if you have taken an excerpt, or cropped a photo... It is not necessary to note trivial alterations, such as correcting a typo or changing a font size." We drop etymology, quotations, translations and so on, so "Excerpted and reformatted" belongs in the full credit. The API also appears to hand us shortened text (the `worship` noun sense "…including magistrates" ends without a full stop; whether the live page is longer is UNVERIFIED), so we may be passing on someone else's excerpt.
4. **Do not imply endorsement.** 4.0 s2(a)(6) bars asserting or implying a connection with, or endorsement by, the licensor; 3.0 s4(c) says the same. The sample full credit says "not endorsed by Wiktionary or the Wikimedia Foundation".
5. **Trademarks are not licensed** (4.0 s2(b)(2): "Patent and trademark rights are not licensed under this Public License."). Wikimedia's Trademark Policy 3.6 lets you use the plain wordmark to describe "A derivative work of Wikimedia content in a way that is not misleading (e.g., "the encyclopedic content on this site is derived from Wikipedia")". So the text "Wiktionary" is fine. The policy also allows marks "as a hyperlink to the Wikimedia sites" with logos only resized, not modified, but a text credit is simpler and keeps us clear of the policy's separate logo notice, so use the word, not a logo.
6. **Where it must appear.** The licence's test is "reasonable... based on the medium, means, and context", and a link to a page that has the details is allowed. My reading: every surface that shows dictionary text carries the short credit (source badge plus tooltip), and the details view carries the full credit, with one site-level licences page behind the links. How little is "reasonable" in a dense grid row is a judgement call, so I would not rely on the Term column alone: show a source badge on each dictionary row. UNVERIFIED how a court or Wikimedia would read a grid with credit only in a tooltip.
7. **If a licensor asks us to remove credit,** 4.0 s3(a)(3) says we must "to the extent reasonably practicable". Wiktionary has not asked for anything beyond the above.
8. **Copyright notice and "title":** Wiktionary supplies no per-entry copyright notice in the entry text; its stated credit mechanism is the page link. 3.0 also asks for "the title of the Work if supplied"; naming the entry ("the Wiktionary entry “worship”") covers it.

## 3. Share-alike and user-written definitions

### 3.1 Is showing a user definition beside a dictionary definition an adaptation? No (practical reading)

- **Share-alike only attaches to adaptations.** CC BY-SA 4.0 s3(b): "In addition to the conditions in Section 3(a), if You Share Adapted Material You produce, the following conditions also apply." The conditions are that the adapter's licence be CC BY-SA (this or a later version) or a compatible one (s3(b)(1)), that we include the licence text or link (s3(b)(2)), and that we impose no extra restrictions (s3(b)(3)).
- **What counts as an adaptation.** s1(a): "material subject to Copyright and Similar Rights that is derived from or based upon the Licensed Material and in which the Licensed Material is translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified in a manner requiring permission under the Copyright and Similar Rights held by the Licensor." Placing another person's text next to Wiktionary's text does not translate, alter, arrange or transform the Wiktionary text. The CC FAQ: "Generally, a modification rises to the level of an adaptation under copyright law when the modified work is based on the prior work but manifests sufficient new creativity to be copyrightable, such as a translation of a novel from one language to another, or the creation of a screenplay based on a novel." Whether something is an adaptation "depends primarily on the applicable copyright law".
- **Collections.** The CC FAQ: "All Creative Commons licenses (including the version 4.0 licenses) allow licensed material to be included in collections such as anthologies, encyclopedias, and broadcasts. You may choose a license for the collection, however this does not change the license applicable to the original material." And: "If the combination does not create an adaptation, then you may combine any CC-licensed content so long as you provide attribution". Its collection table marks CC BY-SA original works as usable in a commercial collection. The 3.0 text is explicit: s1(a) says "a work that constitutes a Collection will not be considered an Adaptation for the purpose of this License", and s4(b) says even an adaptation placed in a Collection "does not require the Collection apart from the Adaptation itself to be made subject to the terms of the Applicable License".
- **So** a definitions list where each item keeps its own text, source label and licence is a collection-style arrangement: the Wiktionary items keep CC BY-SA and the user items keep whatever our Terms give them. Share-alike does not spread sideways.
- **What would weaken this reading** (keep these out of the design): blending dictionary and custom text into one field or one paragraph; a single merged "best definition" built from both; automatic rewording, summarising or "improving" dictionary text (including by the planned Jeeves assistant) and displaying the result; sorting that makes the two look like one undifferentiated list with no source label.

### 3.2 What if a user edits a dictionary definition?

- Changing the wording of a Wiktionary definition and publishing the result is the textbook adaptation: the edited text is "derived from or based upon" the original. Consequences, from the CC FAQ: "the original CC license always applies to the material you are adapting even once adapted... Recipients of the adaptation must comply with both the CC license on the original and your adapter's license", and "when remixing ShareAlike content, your adapter's license must be the same license as the license on the material you are adapting" (or later/compatible). Wikimedia's Terms of Use 7(h) says the same for text from Wikimedia sites: "you agree to license the modified or added content under CC BY-SA 4.0 or later".
- For Perspectize that would mean: the edited definition is offered to everyone under CC BY-SA, carries the credit and a "modified" note, and our Terms cannot add restrictions on it (4.0 s3(b)(3)). That is a different licence on one kind of user content and a UI burden on every edit.
- **Short dictionary definitions may be too thin to be copyrightable**, and Wikimedia's own Terms say facts "may be reused freely without attribution" (7(a)). I would not build on that: it is untested for any specific definition, depends on the country, and complying is cheap.
- **Recommendation:** dictionary definitions are read-only; "write my own" opens a blank box with no prefill from dictionary text. A person who reads a definition and writes their own in their own words is not copying expression (Wiktionary:Copyrights, flagged as an outdated page, says "it is perfectly legal to read a dictionary entry, or other work, reformulate it in your own words"). If the product later wants a "start from this definition" button, the resulting definition has to be a CC BY-SA adaptation with credit and a licence badge, and that is a decision for the owner and the lawyer.
- **Pasted copies.** The map's rule that identical normalized text within one term is the same definition means an exact paste lands on the dictionary row, which keeps the credit. A lightly reworded paste becomes a custom definition with no credit and possibly an uncredited adaptation. We cannot detect that; the Terms should have users warrant that their text is their own and give us a takedown route. Note for ticket 05: store the verbatim text and display it verbatim; normalising for the identity key only is a technical step (4.0 s2(a)(4) says technical modifications "necessary to" exercise the rights never produce Adapted Material), but altering what we show is not.

### 3.3 Perspectives that quote a definition

A perspective that quotes a short dictionary definition with credit is a use of the text with attribution, not a new adaptation of it. How much quotation stays inside "reasonable" without a licence is a copyright question I did not research (UNVERIFIED); the CC FAQ warns that many small excerpts can add up ("if you are using excerpts of CC-licensed material which individually are minimal... but together make up a significant copyrightable chunk, you must comply with the license terms").

### 3.4 The database angle

Sui generis database rights are an EU-style right (CC BY-SA 4.0 s1(l): rights "resulting from Directive 96/9/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 March 1996 on the legal protection of databases... as well as other essentially equivalent rights anywhere in the world"). CC BY-SA 4.0 s4(b): if we put "all or a substantial portion of the database contents in a database in which You have Sui Generis Database Rights, then the database in which You have Sui Generis Database Rights (but not its individual contents) is Adapted Material", which brings in s3(b) when we Share it. The CC FAQ: "the SA licenses require you to apply the same or a compatible license to any database you share publicly and in which you include a substantial portion of the licensed database contents. Note that this does **not** require you to ShareAlike any copyright or other rights you have in the individual contents of the database." And "There is no bright line test for what constitutes a 'substantial portion'... using several insubstantial portions can add up to a substantial portion."

Reading: fetching one entry per term a user adds is not a substantial portion. A bulk import of a dictionary dump (the map's open question) combined with a public bulk export of our table could be, in a jurisdiction that recognises these rights. Even then only the database as a whole is affected, not user-written definitions in it. Whether we hold such rights, and where, is for the lawyer.

### 3.5 Our own Terms of Service and the app

- 4.0 s2(a)(5)(c): "You may not offer or impose any additional or different terms or conditions on, or apply any Effective Technological Measures to, the Licensed Material if doing so restricts exercise of the Licensed Rights by any recipient." So a Terms clause such as "you may not copy or scrape content from the service" must carve out Wiktionary text and the audio.
- Requiring sign-in is fine. The CC FAQ: "you may post material under any CC license on a site restricted to members... or to paying customers, but you may not place effective technological measures (including DRM) on the files that prevents them from sharing the material elsewhere." Nothing in the plan looks like an ETM.
- Do not license Wiktionary text onward under our Terms (4.0 licences are non-sublicensable, s2(a)(1)); each recipient gets an offer straight from the licensor (s2(a)(5)(a)). Add a statement to the Terms and a licences page: "Dictionary content is © its authors under CC BY-SA and is not covered by these Terms."
- The GraphQL API is how the text reaches clients, so return the source URL, licence name/URL and retrieved date next to every dictionary definition. That lets any consuming client attribute properly.

## 4. Snapshot in our database vs fetching live

**Same obligations.** The licence regulates "Share", defined as providing material to the public "such as reproduction, public display... dissemination, communication" (s1(k)), and it grants the right to "reproduce and Share the Licensed Material, in whole or in part" (s2(a)(1)(a)). Whether the bytes sit in Postgres or arrive per request, we are reproducing and displaying them to users, so the credit, licence link, modification note and no-extra-restrictions rules are identical.

**Where a snapshot differs:**

| Point | Snapshot in our DB | Live fetch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attribution data | Must be stored with the text, because the page that proves it may change: source URL, retrieval date, licence name and URL as returned. A revision id would pin the exact text (the Free Dictionary API does not return one, UNVERIFIED that any route to it is cheap). | Comes from the response each time. |
| Wiktionary edits or deletes the entry | Our copy stays lawful: the licence is "irrevocable" (s2(a)(1)) and s6(c) says the licensor stopping distribution "will not terminate this Public License". The copy goes stale, and if Wiktionary removed text because it infringed someone else's copyright, our copy would keep it. s5 disclaims any warranty of title, so the licence offers no protection against that. Need a refresh and a takedown path (map: "Refreshing a stored dictionary entry"). | Always current; a removal disappears at once. |
| "Substantial portion of a database" | Only if we accumulate or bulk-import a large share and publish it (section 3.4). | Not applicable unless we cache wholesale. |
| Third-party service | None after the fetch. | Depends on `api.dictionaryapi.dev` staying up; its `x-ratelimit-limit: 450` header has no stated window; no terms found. Calling Wikimedia's own APIs needs an identifying User-Agent with contact details ("Scripts should use an informative User-Agent string with contact information, or they may be blocked without notice") and Wikimedia's rate limits; in this session Wiktionary's action API answered "You are making too many requests to the API" and its REST/prefix APIs returned 429 (map). That is operational, not a licence term. |

The map's plan (fetch once when a term is added, store with an "as of" date and credit) is compatible with the licence. Record the retrieval date in the credit as proposed.

## 5. Pronunciation audio

**Yes, it is licensed separately, per file.**

- Wikimedia Terms of Use 7(d): "Non-text media on the Projects are available under a variety of different licenses". 7(g): "For any non-text media, you agree to comply with the applicable license under which the work has been made available (which can be discovered by clicking on the work and looking at the licensing section on its description page...)". Wiktionary:Copyrights: material from outside "retain their rights" and reusers "must comply with the separate copyright terms for that material as well". Commons' own guidance (Commons:Reusing content outside Wikimedia): confirm the licence suits you, attribute the creator ("it is the content creator who must be credited, not the uploader"), "Specify license details", and "If the file is available under multiple licenses, you can use any of them".
- **What the Free Dictionary API returned (probed 2026-11-14):**

| Word | Audio file served | Source page named | Licence given by the API |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| worship | `api.dictionaryapi.dev/media/pronunciations/en/worship-us.mp3` | `commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=1676909` | BY-SA 3.0 |
| hello (file `-au`) | `.../hello-au.mp3` | `...curid=75797336` | BY-SA 4.0 |
| hello (file `-uk`) | `.../hello-uk.mp3` | `...curid=9021983` | BY 3.0 US |
| mercy | `.../mercy-us.mp3` | `...curid=192266` | BY-SA 3.0 |

  Some phonetic entries have `"audio": ""` and no licence (for example the first `worship` and `mercy` entries, and the only `bliss` entry). The licence object sits on the audio entry, separate from the entry-level `"license":{"name":"CC BY-SA 3.0"}`.
- **The author is not in the API payload.** For `worship` I resolved `curid=1676909` through the Commons API: `File:En-us-worship.ogg`, uploaded 2007, Artist "Dvortygirl" (`User:Dvortygirl`), `LicenseShortName` "CC BY-SA 3.0", `AttributionRequired` "true", `Copyrighted` "True". The file's Categories also list GFDL and CC-BY-SA 2.5/2.0/1.0 with a note "Dual licensed per GFDL Version 1.2 or later / Creative Commons Attribution ShareAlike license versions 2.5, 2.0, and 1.0 as noted", so the Commons metadata and its own description wording disagree about the CC version; the API's "BY-SA 3.0" matches Commons' `LicenseShortName`. Since Commons guidance says any of several offered licences may be used, crediting under the one the API and Commons metadata name is fine. To credit properly we need to fetch the author from Commons when we snapshot, or credit "Wikimedia Commons" with a link to the file page (4.0 s3(a)(2) lets a link to a page holding the details stand in).
- **Credit format.** Commons:Credit line is an essay, not policy, but it shows the accepted form: author / "Wikimedia Commons" / licence link, e.g. "John Doe / [CC-BY-SA-3.0]". For us: "Pronunciation: Dvortygirl via Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0", with the author linked to their Commons user page, "Wikimedia Commons" to the file page, and the licence name to its deed. For CC BY 3.0 US files there is no share-alike, but attribution is still required. For public-domain or CC0 files credit is not required but is good practice (UNVERIFIED which licences occur beyond the three seen).
- **Commons licences allow commercial use.** Commons:Licensing: "Commercial use of the work must be allowed" and non-commercial licences "are not accepted". So audio hosted on Commons is not NC-restricted. Audio on Wiktionary itself rather than Commons, or hosted elsewhere, would need its own check.
- **Format and edits.** The API serves an mp3 it hosts, while Commons holds an `.ogg`. A change of format is not an adaptation (CC FAQ: those changes "are not considered adaptations even if applicable law would suggest otherwise"). Trimming, looping, mixing or layering the audio would be a modification we have to indicate, and may be an adaptation that triggers share-alike. Plan to play it as served.
- **Hot-linking vs storing.** Hot-linking `api.dictionaryapi.dev/media/...` depends on a third-party host; storing our own copy needs the author, licence and file URL stored with it and the credit shown next to the play control. Either way the credit must be reachable from where the audio plays.
- The phonetic text (IPA) is part of the entry and is covered by the text credit.

## 6. Does calling Perspectize commercial change anything?

- **Not for Wiktionary's text.** CC BY-SA 4.0 lists its licence elements as "Attribution and ShareAlike" (s1(g)); there is no NonCommercial restriction. The CC FAQ's collection table marks BY-SA works as usable in a commercial collection. Wikimedia's Terms of Use 7(a): "these licenses do allow commercial uses of your contributions, as long as such uses are compliant with the terms of the respective licenses."
- **Where commercial status could matter:**
  1. **Fair-use material inside Wiktionary.** Wiktionary:Copyrights: entries "may include text, images, sounds, or other material from external sources with different copyright terms, and which is used with permission or under the 'fair use' doctrine", and a fair-use image's reuse "might not be" fair use "if you were using a Wiktionary article for a commercial use that would otherwise be allowed by CC-BY-SA". Wikimedia's dump licence page adds that some content is included under fair use and "Use of this information may be a copyright violation if used in a different context than they are used on Wikipedia." Wiktionary quotations are the likely carrier. We saw one in the API data: for `mercy`, a usage `example` is "Psalms 40:11 Do not withhold Your tender mercies from me, O Lord", which reads like a modern Bible translation (which translation, and whether it is in copyright, is UNVERIFIED). **Recommendation:** store only definition text, part of speech and phonetics for now; leave out `example` strings, quotations and etymology until someone confirms where they come from. This matters most for a scripture-adjacent product.
  2. **Trademarks** (section 2, point 5): not licensed by CC BY-SA, commercial or not.
  3. **Third-party API terms.** The Free Dictionary API publishes no terms, so we cannot say what it allows for commercial use; the licence on the text does not bind the API's own service terms. That is the reliability question in ticket 03.
  4. **Warranty.** s5: the licensor offers the material "as-is and as-available" and disclaims accuracy; it does not change with commercial use, but a paid product that presents a definition as authoritative should say it is a Wiktionary excerpt.

## 7. What this means for the design

For Decision 9 (source terms), tickets 03, 05 and 10:

1. **Decision 9 outcome:** "may store", with conditions: credit, licence link, modification note, no endorsement, no logos.
2. **Data to store per dictionary entry:** source page URL, retrieved-at date, licence name and URL as returned by the API, plus the term it belongs to. Per audio file: audio URL, Commons file page, author, licence name and URL.
3. **Separation rule (Decision 9 / ticket 10):** each dictionary definition carries a visible source badge ("From Wiktionary · CC BY-SA 4.0"); dictionary rows are read-only; custom definitions are a different kind of row with their own label; no merged text; no prefilled edit; the full credit appears once per term in the details view; the Add preview (Decision 8) shows the same credit.
4. **Terms of Service:** carve-out for third-party content; user warranty of originality for custom definitions; takedown route.
5. **GraphQL:** expose `sourceUrl`, `licenseName`, `licenseUrl`, `retrievedAt` (and audio author/licence) with dictionary definitions.
6. **Not stored for now:** `example` strings, quotations, etymology, translations (unconfirmed third-party material).
